# Awakening Earth: Institutions, Economy & Society

**Status:** Canonical design source for Issue #24 — Institutions, Economy & Society  
**Scope:** Reusable setting-level societal rules and defaults for *Awakening Earth*. Generated campaigns derive concrete local institutions from these constraints rather than assuming one canonical town.

## Core Principle

Modern society persists.

Jobs, rent, hospitals, insurance, emergency services, police, firms, schools, social media, ordinary markets, families, and local government still exist. They adapt around supernatural pressures rather than being replaced by a mature fantasy "hunter society."

Institutions should exist because recurring pressures create incentives and obligations for them to exist.

## Monster Response Ecology

The normal civilian response to a monster is still to call public emergency services.

Public dispatch coordinates incidents even when private responders eventually handle the kill.

### Weak threats: public response

Weak monsters are primarily a public-safety responsibility.

Public monster-response units may include:

- awakened responders;
- mundane responders equipped with magical weapons or items;
- evacuation, rescue, perimeter, medical, intelligence, and scene-control personnel.

Repeated successful monster combat can eventually awaken mundane responders, creating a natural personnel pipeline.

Loot generated by kills performed on duty belongs to the public agency or government under ordinary employment terms and can help fund response, equipment, training, and growth.

### Medium threats: independents and small teams

Medium threats are the natural independent-contractor niche:

- too dangerous for routine public response;
- often not lucrative enough to justify mobilizing a major firm.

This is a major player-scale adventure space.

Independents retain freedom and ordinary loot rights but bear their own travel, equipment, liability, medical, and business costs.

### Major threats: large private firms

Large threats with high expected loot value support major private response firms.

Firms can provide:

- training;
- equipment;
- logistics;
- medical access;
- teammates;
- legal and administrative support;
- better access to buyers and markets.

Employment contracts commonly assign some or all on-the-clock loot rights to the firm.

## Combat Capability and Range

Not all awakened powers are combat-oriented.

Awakened people impart enough magic to objects they are directly wielding to let those objects interact meaningfully with magical resistance. A non-combat awakener with a melee weapon can therefore threaten weak monsters.

That does **not** make every awakener a capable monster hunter. Stronger monsters can overwhelm someone whose powers, attributes, training, equipment, or team are inadequate.

Mundane ranged weapons are less effective because an awakener's ambient magical reinforcement dissipates after projectiles leave their proximity.

Serious ranged combat still exists through:

- ranged powers;
- magical ranged weapons or ammunition;
- abilities that preserve or project magical influence at distance.

The exact mechanical realization belongs to the reference ruleset and Awakening Earth adapter.

## Loot and Monster Death

Monsters normally disintegrate after death.

Their death resolves into **loot** rather than leaving an ordinary harvestable carcass.

Loot may include:

- finished magical items;
- consumables;
- magical materials or components;
- unusual artifacts;
- research objects or resources.

By default, loot belongs to the person or people who ended the threat unless a contract assigns those rights elsewhere.

Some powers can prevent or delay disintegration. Doing so also prevents the normal loot manifestation.

This creates an opportunity-cost tradeoff:

- take the loot; or
- preserve an intact monster for research.

Governments, universities, firms, or laboratories may pay enough for preserved specimens to compensate responders for sacrificed expected loot.

Ownership does not imply unrestricted sale. Dangerous magical items, toxins, artifacts, or other hazardous drops may be regulated.

Most independents sell through appraisers, brokers, dealers, processors, researchers, or auctions. Large firms can vertically integrate more of this market and therefore extract more value from the same kill.

## Advancement and License Colors

Nobody awakens already powerful.

People begin near the bottom and grow through supernatural progression.

Crossing thresholds strengthens the person broadly, including body, mind, and powers.

Responder licenses use broad color bands based on supernatural advancement:

**Red → Orange → Yellow → Green → Blue → Purple → Pink → Silver → Gold**

These are bands, not one-color-per-threshold.

Gold exists as a future-facing classification. The first playable era has no Gold awakeners, and upper bands may be extremely rare or theoretical.

License color represents **supernatural advancement**, not total competence or job fitness.

## Training and Certification

Training and license color are separate.

Color answers:

> How far has this person progressed supernaturally?

Professional certification answers:

> What is this person trained and authorized to do?

There is no universal supernatural academy.

Training remains decentralized and recognizably modern:

- public agencies train public responders;
- firms train employees;
- independents use private courses, gyms, combat schools, experienced mentors, ranges, and specialist certifications.

Baseline responder certification exists primarily to establish that someone can operate responsibly at an emergency scene, including relevant first aid, communications, evacuation, magical-item safety, and monster-response fundamentals.

Training improves competence but does not itself produce supernatural progression.

## Magical Medicine and Healthcare

Ordinary medicine remains important.

Magical medical effects fall into distinct categories.

### Regeneration

Accelerates growth, tissue repair, and replacement.

### Cleansing or purification

Removes harmful foreign substances or effects.

### Restoration

Attempts to return a system toward an earlier or healthier state.

### Curative effects

Target a specific pathological process.

Regeneration is powerful but not intelligent.

Strong regenerative healing, especially healing capable of replacing large amounts of tissue or entire limbs, carries meaningful biological risk:

- increased carcinogenic risk from extreme cell growth and division;
- poisons and harmful biological processes may also be accelerated;
- repeated exposure may be medically concerning.

This is not "use a potion and get cancer." It is a risk significant enough that clinicians and responders do not automatically reach for regenerative healing first.

Disease is not necessarily damage.

Generic regeneration does not automatically cure cancer, Alzheimer's disease, anemia, diabetes, genetic disorders, or other underlying pathologies. Exact magical interactions with those diseases remain intentionally open for future setting work.

Magical healing supplements rather than replaces hospitals, diagnostics, surgery, pharmacology, rehabilitation, and ordinary medicine.

## Insurance and Property Damage

Monster damage becomes an ordinary insurable risk.

Coverage and premiums respond to:

- local monster frequency;
- severity history;
- response capacity;
- local institutional reliability.

Communities with weak response capacity can become more expensive to insure even when an individual policyholder has never personally encountered a monster.

Responder-caused collateral damage is evaluated differently depending on whether it was:

- necessary and reasonable during authorized emergency response;
- negligent;
- reckless.

Public responders are covered through government structures.

Major firms carry organizational liability coverage.

Independent responders generally need professional liability coverage.

Catastrophic incidents may exceed ordinary private-insurance capacity, so broader government disaster or reinsurance backstops exist as setting truth without needing detailed first-slice simulation.

## Media, Reputation, and Public Opinion

There is no single public attitude toward awakened people.

They may be seen as:

- public servants;
- contractors;
- local heroes;
- celebrities;
- influencers;
- reckless thrill-seekers;
- dangerous people;
- rich opportunists;
- necessary protectors.

Social media, livestreams, forums, sponsorships, firm PR, eyewitness footage, amateur analysis, and local news all shape understanding.

Reputation is particularly **local and event-driven**.

An independent team may become deeply trusted in one community while remaining almost unknown nationally.

Media incentives can distort responder behavior, firm branding, sponsorships, and public perception.

## Class and Economic Effects

Existing class structures do not disappear.

Supernatural advancement creates a new axis of inequality layered on top of ordinary wealth, education, connections, geography, and institutional access.

Most awakened people do **not** become rich.

Successful responders can enter a compounding loop:

**survive fights → progress → access stronger threats → acquire more valuable loot → gain better resources → improve future survival and earning potential**

Large firms are especially effective at capturing this compounding value.

Public responders may trade upside for stability and benefits.

Firm employees may earn well while surrendering significant loot rights.

Independents can retain upside but absorb much more risk and overhead.

Color becomes a visible status signal, but it does not imply wealth, ethics, professionalism, education, or competence.

## Legal Status and Crime

Awakening does not create a separate legal caste.

The law primarily regulates **activities**, not the mere fact that someone has powers.

Examples:

- harmless personal powers require no universal registration;
- professional monster response requires licensing or certification;
- hazardous magical items may require special handling or permits;
- dangerous loot may have sale or transport restrictions;
- powers used to commit ordinary crimes remain governed by ordinary criminal law.

Teleporting into a locked building is still burglary.

Supernatural coercion used for fraud or extortion remains fraud or extortion.

Super-strength assault remains assault.

Universal mandatory registration of all awakened people is not a default setting rule.

## Law Enforcement and Long-Term State Power

Early in the setting, ordinary policing remains broadly functional because awakened people begin weak.

Law enforcement can adapt through:

- magical equipment;
- awakened officers;
- specialized tactical teams;
- mutual aid;
- cooperation with public response units;
- contracted assistance when necessary.

Over time, progression creates a structural tension:

**legal authority and practically enforceable authority may diverge.**

Eventually some awakened people may become physically difficult for ordinary institutions to restrain even when the law is unambiguous.

This is a gradual historical development, not an immediate collapse of state authority.

## Setting Truth, Simulation State, and Generated Campaign Content

### Setting truth and background structure

The reusable setting owns:

- response ecology;
- loot ownership defaults;
- license color system;
- training and certification distinctions;
- magical-medicine categories;
- broad insurance and liability norms;
- ordinary criminal-law continuity;
- market and firm incentives;
- progression-driven inequality;
- long-term state-enforcement tension.

### Stateful systems when locally relevant

A campaign may need mutable state for:

- local public-response capacity;
- responder or team availability;
- active incidents and assignments;
- loot ownership, value, and restrictions;
- injuries and recovery;
- relevant insurance or liability disputes;
- player or team reputation;
- local firm and independent competition;
- local market access.

### Generated campaign content

Concrete local manifestations belong to campaign content, including:

- agencies and offices;
- named responders;
- hospitals and clinics;
- insurers;
- dealers and appraisers;
- firms;
- independent teams;
- local media personalities;
- exact response times and capacity;
- local economic pressures.

## Region-Generation Defaults

These institutions are **generative constraints and defaults**, not a mandate to author one canonical town.

A generated campaign region should derive its local institutional footprint from facts such as:

- settlement size and type;
- population;
- geography and remoteness;
- wealth and tax base;
- nearby population centers;
- monster pressure and history;
- local response capacity;
- transportation access;
- existing private-firm coverage.

A medium city and an isolated rural town should instantiate very different local versions of the same setting-level systems.

## Verification Scenario

A medium-strength monster appears at a local business.

1. Someone calls public emergency services.
2. Public responders evacuate civilians, control the scene, and assess the threat.
3. The monster exceeds routine local capability.
4. A major firm may decline because mobilization cost exceeds likely loot value.
5. Dispatch opens the incident to appropriately licensed independents.
6. The player team accepts and kills the monster.
7. The monster disintegrates and manifests loot.
8. Because the team is independent and has no conflicting contract, the loot belongs to them.
9. A team member suffers a serious injury and must choose between ordinary recovery and scarce or risky magical treatment.
10. Property damage produces an insurance or liability consequence.
11. The team chooses whether to keep, quickly sell, appraise, transport, or otherwise monetize the loot.
12. The outcome affects local reputation and future opportunities.

The scenario exercises public response, private contracting, licensing, loot economics, medicine, insurance, law, and reputation without requiring a national-scale simulation.

## Downstream Boundaries

This document does not define:

- exact local agencies, companies, hospitals, or NPCs;
- exact market prices;
- exact license threshold cutoffs inside each color band;
- exact magical-healing formulas;
- exact legal text in every jurisdiction;
- the final mechanical implementation of magical resistance;
- the generated starting region itself.

Those belong to generated campaign content, ruleset or adapter integration, or later focused setting work.
