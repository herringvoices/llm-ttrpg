# Awakening Earth: Setting & World Premise

**Status:** Canonical design source for Issue #23 — Setting & World Premise  
**Scope:** Foundational setting truth for the *Awakening Earth* setting package. This document defines the reality underlying the setting, not the mechanics used to implement it.

## Core Premise

A few months before the default campaign begins, Earth became a magical world.

The change was instantaneous and planet-wide. Humanity did not see a wave of magic spread across the globe, nor did anyone observe an obvious source. Monsters began appearing, some people began developing supernatural powers, and magical materials and phenomena began emerging throughout ordinary life.

Humanity does not know why this happened.

The hidden truth is that Earth was deliberately awakened by an established extraterrestrial civilization. They dropped a magical seed, roughly the size of Manhattan, into the Pacific Ocean. The seed reached the planet's core and acted as an on switch, causing Earth to become magically active.

The extraterrestrials responsible will not appear in the reference game's normal scope. Their existence and motives are deep setting truth, not ordinary campaign information.

## Magic

Magic is fundamental.

It is not a form of technology, an undiscovered branch of conventional physics, or a resource invented by some civilization. Magic exists in the universe in the same basic sense that matter and space exist.

Magic is unusually flexible and disorderly. It has an inherent tendency toward:

- movement,
- change,
- life,
- and chaos.

Sentient brains and souls can impose some degree of order on that chaos. This is part of what allows intelligent beings to develop coherent supernatural powers rather than merely being transformed unpredictably by ambient magic.

The setting does not need a deeper explanation for what magic "really is." It is magic.

## Planetary Awakening

Older magical civilizations know how to awaken worlds that are not yet magically active.

They do this by planting a magical seed within the planet. On Earth, the seed was deposited in the Pacific Ocean, reached the planetary core, and activated the entire world at once.

Earth's awakening was deliberate.

The civilizations that perform planetary awakenings present the practice as altruistic uplift: they are giving younger civilizations access to magic and, eventually, to a wider magical cosmos.

That claim is not entirely false.

It is also self-serving.

Newly awakened worlds are dangerous environments full of young monsters, unstable magic, and opportunities for rapid growth. Established magical civilizations need such worlds so that their own younger and weaker members have suitable places to become stronger.

They therefore expected Earth to produce monsters. The danger created by planetary awakening is not an accident or an unforeseen side effect.

## Human Awakening

Humans can awaken supernatural powers as Earth becomes saturated with magic.

At the campaign start, awakened humans are globally rare but becoming steadily more common. A useful initial estimate is around **0.5% of the population**, though the exact value may be tuned later if needed for world-scale consistency.

Humans are anomalous compared with other known intelligent species.

For most extraterrestrial species, magical development follows a predictable species-specific sequence. Members of the same species awaken essentially the same powers in the same order.

Humans do not.

Human powers are highly individualized. Different people can develop radically different combinations of abilities with no obvious shared progression path.

This is a genuine peculiarity of humanity, not merely a gap in human understanding.

## Monsters and Magical Life

Monsters have more than one origin.

### Transformed Terrestrial Life

Existing Earth organisms can absorb or interact with magic strongly enough to become something new.

A mundane animal, plant, fungus, or other organism may transform into a magical organism or monster. The rage-tail described in *Quests for Breakfast*, for example, is an ordinary raccoon that becomes a monster.

### Spontaneous Generation

Some monsters and magical organisms arise without a mundane ancestor.

Because magic tends toward movement, life, and chaos, sufficiently active or concentrated magic can produce living or life-like entities spontaneously.

Or, less formally: monsters can arise **spawntaneously**.

### Growth

Monsters can become more dangerous the longer they survive. This phenomenon is observable by humans and contributes to the urgency of dealing with local monster populations before they become substantially harder to manage.

## Magical Resistance

Magic interacts much more effectively with magic than the mundane interacts with magic.

Magical creatures, awakened people, magical materials, and magical effects possess an intrinsic resistance to nonmagical force.

This is a property of the setting, not merely a special defensive ability possessed by monsters.

Conversely, magical attacks and magically empowered actions can interact with magical targets much more normally. An awakened person can therefore harm a monster far more effectively than an otherwise comparable mundane person.

This effect can extend through objects being wielded or directly empowered by an awakened person. The underlying fictional rule is broader than "monsters have damage reduction":

> **Magic resists the mundane. Magic can meaningfully interact with magic.**

The exact numerical implementation of this principle belongs to the ruleset and setting adapter rather than this setting document.

## The World at Campaign Start

The default campaign begins only a few months after Earth's awakening.

Society has not collapsed.

People still:

- go to work,
- attend school,
- pay rent,
- date,
- argue with family,
- worry about taxes,
- run businesses,
- use social media,
- call the police,
- and live mostly recognizable modern lives.

The supernatural has been layered onto ordinary life rather than replacing it.

Institutions are adapting in real time. Governments, private companies, researchers, online communities, contractors, influencers, criminals, insurers, employers, and ordinary households are all trying to understand and respond to a phenomenon nobody anticipated.

Combat is therefore only one possible consequence of the setting. Magical powers can affect jobs, relationships, crime, healthcare, economics, transportation, research, entertainment, and mundane personal problems just as readily as they affect monster hunting.

## Frequency of Supernatural Incidents

Monsters are uncommon at the start of the game, but their frequency is increasing.

For an ordinary person who is not actively looking for supernatural trouble, a useful baseline is:

- **Rural setting:** personally encountering a monster roughly once a month.
- **Urban setting:** personally encountering a monster roughly once every two months.

These are experiential baselines rather than rigid spawn schedules.

Urban areas generally have more organized response capacity and a greater concentration of awakened people. Rural communities can therefore face dangerous gaps in protection even when their raw population density is lower.

## What Humanity Knows

Humanity has no meaningful knowledge of the alien intervention, the planetary seed, or the wider magical civilizations.

A few months of observation have nevertheless established a growing body of practical knowledge.

People broadly understand that:

- magic is real, or at least that "magic" is a useful name for the phenomenon;
- monsters and human powers began appearing during the same period;
- ordinary Earth organisms can transform into monsters;
- some monsters or magical phenomena appear without an obvious mundane source;
- awakened humans develop highly varied powers;
- magical beings are unusually resistant to mundane harm;
- awakened people and magical effects can harm monsters much more effectively;
- magical objects and materials exist;
- monsters can produce valuable magical materials or items;
- monsters can grow substantially stronger if they survive long enough;
- and the frequency of both awakenings and monster incidents is increasing.

Humanity does **not** have a unified explanation for why any of this is happening.

Scientists, governments, hobbyists, religious communities, online forums, conspiracy theorists, journalists, and ordinary people all build competing explanations from incomplete evidence.

The *Quests for Breakfast* manuscript is the primary tonal reference for what ordinary people currently know and how quickly public knowledge is developing.

## Terminology and Public Culture

Because the Awakening is only a few months old, terminology is still developing rather than being inherited from an ancient supernatural society.

Informal language, internet slang, government terminology, contractor jargon, academic language, and community-specific labels can coexist.

The setting should not assume that mature genre institutions such as standardized adventurer ranks, universal guild systems, or globally agreed classifications already exist unless later design explicitly establishes them.

Public understanding is messy, fast-moving, partially correct, and socially distributed.

## Extraterrestrial Context

Relevant extraterrestrial civilizations are broadly humanoid.

This follows intentionally simplified "Star Trek rules": intelligent aliens may differ substantially in appearance, culture, biology, and magical development, but the setting does not require radically non-humanoid body plans for its important intelligent species.

This is both a setting choice and a practical constraint that keeps characters legible to players and LLMs.

The extraterrestrials themselves are outside the normal scope of the first reference campaign. Detailed alien civilizations, politics, species catalogs, and their reactions to humanity's unusual awakening are not required for the first playable game.

## Canonical Hidden Truth vs. Public Knowledge

The setting must maintain a strict distinction between objective truth and what characters know.

### Canonical Hidden Truth

- Magic is a fundamental feature of reality.
- Earth was deliberately awakened.
- A massive magical seed was dropped into the Pacific and reached the planet's core.
- Established magical civilizations caused the Awakening.
- They expected monsters to appear.
- Newly awakened worlds are valuable growth environments for weaker members of established magical civilizations.
- Their rhetoric of altruistic uplift is incomplete because it conceals this self-interest.
- Human power development is genuinely unusual compared with other known intelligent species.

### Publicly Observable or Widely Known

- Magic and monsters appeared a few months ago.
- The event affected the entire planet.
- Some humans awaken powers.
- Some Earth organisms transform into monsters.
- Other magical organisms and phenomena can appear without an obvious mundane precursor.
- Magical things resist mundane harm.
- Magic is effective against magic.
- Monsters can become stronger over time.
- Awakenings and supernatural incidents are becoming more common.

### Unknown to Humanity

- The existence of the planetary seed.
- The alien origin of Earth's Awakening.
- The intentional purpose behind creating dangerous newly awakened worlds.
- The predictable species-wide power progressions found in other intelligent species.
- The fact that human individualized power development is anomalous on a galactic scale.

## Setting Constraints for the Engine and LLM

The LLM may interpret evidence, portray beliefs, and narrate uncertainty, but it must not invent competing metaphysical truths.

New supernatural content must remain compatible with the following foundational rules:

1. All supernatural phenomena ultimately participate in the same fundamental magic.
2. Earth became magically active because of the planetary seed.
3. Magic tends toward movement, life, change, and chaos.
4. Sentient minds and souls can impose structure on magic.
5. Humans manifest unusually individualized power sets.
6. Monsters may be transformed Earth life or arise spontaneously from magical activity.
7. Magical things resist nonmagical force.
8. Humanity does not know the extraterrestrial cause of the Awakening.
9. Ordinary modern life continues alongside supernatural disruption.
10. The setting should support social, economic, occupational, investigative, relational, environmental, and supernatural play without making combat the default structure.

## Downstream Design Boundaries

This document establishes setting reality but does not define:

- exact mechanical resolution of magical resistance;
- exact character progression mathematics;
- the final awakening-rate balance;
- detailed institutions, law, economics, insurance, or monster-response systems;
- the player's specific starting relationship to the Awakening;
- the concrete first playable town or region;
- detailed extraterrestrial cultures or politics;
- or a comprehensive taxonomy of monsters, powers, or anomalies.

Those belong to the appropriate downstream ruleset, adapter, campaign, or setting-design issues.
